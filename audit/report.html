FINDING 1Speed: fine on a desktop, painful on a phone

Google measures how long until a page's main content is visible ("Largest Contentful Paint"). Under 2.5 seconds is good; over 4 is failing.

│ marker = 2.5 s, Google's threshold for a "good" experience [1]

It isn't only phones: a real Chrome session on a desktop Mac measured 9.3 s before the first content appeared — rated "Poor" on every Google speed measure except layout stability.[9]

A customer who Googles a builder waits three to four times longer than Google's limit. Slow sites lose visitors before they see a single project — and Google ranks them lower because of it.

The page is heavy, and nothing is saved for the next visit

This homepage 3.2 MB
A well-built site ≈1 MB

Oversized photos make up most of the weight, and the server sends no instruction to remember them — so even returning visitors re-download everything, every time.[1][6]

This is the single biggest fix. Properly sized photos and modern hosting would cut the page to a third of its size and take the 8-second wait to under 2.

The server itself is slow and unpredictable

Two independent real-visit measurements clocked the server at 2.8 seconds just to hand over the page — 99.9% of that is the server "thinking", not the network — while a lab test the same evening got an instant response.[7][9][1] Google rates anything over 1.8 s as "Poor".

This is what budget shared hosting looks like from the outside: sometimes fine, sometimes slow, with no way to know which a customer will get.

Under the bonnet: assembled from too many pieces

The homepage pulls in 27 separate stylesheet files and 16 scripts over an older connection standard (HTTP/1.1) that makes them queue single-file.[9][6] The big "Teamwork — built on excellence" banner — the first thing on the page — isn't marked as a priority, and two of the images at the top are explicitly told to load late.[9] 11 of the 13 images have no text description, which search engines and screen readers rely on.[9]

None of this is the content's fault — it's assembly. A rebuild ships one stylesheet, prioritises the banner, and describes every photo; the same content, delivered properly.

FINDING 2Being found: the site only ranks for its own name

A website earns its keep when people who don't know the company yet can find it.

✕ Critical

Google is told the site is about… "Techni Construction – Techni Construction"

That is the site's actual headline in Google's index — the brand name twice, with no mention of construction company, builders, Port Elizabeth or Gqeberha.[4]

Someone searching "construction companies Port Elizabeth" finds directory sites — which list Techni next to its competitors — instead of Techni's own site. The company is a line item on other people's pages.

▲ Needs work

Facebook: 1,978 followers, nobody home

The page has a real audience but almost no activity ("2 people talking about this") — and a second, duplicate Techni Construction page (≈1,700 likes) splits that audience in two.[5]

Nearly 3,700 combined followers is a genuine asset most competitors don't have — currently going unused.

✕ Critical

The Google Maps listing is working against the company

For a local contractor, the Google Maps listing usually brings more enquiries than the website itself. Techni's listing:[10]

· Appears unclaimed ("Own this business?" is showing) — anyone can suggest edits to it.
· Its Website button sends people to Facebook — a link that now dead-ends in a redirect — instead of techniconstruction.co.za.
· 4.1★ from 11 reviews; the newest review (~10 months ago) is a 1★, and no review has ever received a reply.
· 3 photos in total — the cover image a searcher sees is a photo of a branded cake from six years ago.
· The address on Maps doesn't match the address on the website (different suburb and postcode).

This is the highest-return fix on this entire report and it costs nothing but time: reclaim the listing, point the button at the website, load 20–30 project photos, and start asking happy clients for reviews.

THE POINTWhat a rebuilt site changes

The people who hire a CIDB 7 GB contractor — architects, quantity surveyors, developers, serious private clients — check the website when they shortlist. Today it answers a fraction of their questions and asks them to phone for the rest. Every gap is a place to quietly lose a job.
